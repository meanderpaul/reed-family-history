Reed Family History

A working record of the Reed name, kept by the family. The line is confirmed from Capt. Joseph Read of Westford, Massachusetts, down through John Reed of Stoddard, New Hampshire, to the present day: Westford's vital records carry the generations from Joseph to Isaiah, and the family's Stoddard documents name John as Isaiah's son. Above Joseph the line rests on the published genealogies, running back to Thomas Read, the King Philip's War captive at the top of the line, whose parentage is the family's brick wall. This site keeps what is proven apart from what is still a lead, so nobody mistakes one for the other.

The story, in brief

A captive in King Philip's War. The family's story opens in 1676 with Thomas Read, a tailor of Chelmsford, Massachusetts, serving as a soldier in King Philip's War. Taken captive, he met Mary Rowlandson in the camps, and she wrote him into her famous narrative, in tears and fearing he would be killed. On May 15, 1676, Thomas escaped and reached Hatfield, carrying word of the encampments near the falls of the Connecticut River. Four days later, Captain William Turner's force attacked the encampment at Peskeompskut, remembered as the Battle of Turner's Falls. Thomas lived on as a Chelmsford freeman and tailor, and died sometime between 1730 and 1736. Who his parents were, no record found so far says. He is the top of the line, and the brick wall.

A century in Westford. Thomas's descendants stayed in Massachusetts. His son Thomas Jr. married into Westford and died there in 1773. His grandson, Capt. Joseph Read, became a central figure in Westford's civic and political life in the Revolutionary period: the town's historical society counts him a Boston Tea Party participant, a claim the Tea Party museum's researchers dispute, so it stays flagged on this site. Joseph's son Joshua answered the Lexington alarm on April 19, 1775, marching in Capt. Jonathan Minot's company on the first day of the Revolutionary War.

North to Stoddard. Joshua's son Isaiah, born in Westford on March 17, 1778, married Lydia Procter there on February 12, 1799, and moved north to Stoddard, a hill town in Cheshire County, New Hampshire, where their seven children were born. The town history knows him as "Isaiah Reed, Esq." His later years had an edge to them: in 1848 fire took two dwelling houses owned by Isaiah and a son, Lydia died in 1857 and he remarried, and late in life Stoddard's Congregational church excommunicated him for slipping away to Universalist meetings, while his son Jonas P. sat as a deacon of that same church. Isaiah died on March 13, 1868, and his will, proved that year, names his children. Among them is John.

The Jackson Hill farm. John Reed bought his homestead on top of Jackson Hill in 1842: some 175 acres for $2,000, at 2,060 feet above sea level. He farmed it with his wife Harriet, and the inventory taken after his death lists 13 oxen, a cow, 10 sheep and 85 sap buckets, a working hill farm that made maple sugar to sell as well as to eat. John died of typhoid on October 5, 1866, aged 49. Harriet bought the farm back in 1869 and held it for two more decades, and the place stayed in Reed hands for 58 years in all. The house is long gone, but when Arlene Rasmussen walked the site in 1976 the L-shaped cellar hole was still there, with the chimney bricks piled inside it, the barn's stone walls across the road, and the family's old apple trees at the fork of the dirt road.

Out of the hills. John's son James Edwin farmed in Stoddard like his father, and raised nine children there with Abby Wells. Their son William, born in Stoddard in 1892 and registered at birth as "Willie," carried the line into the twentieth century: his 1942 draft registration places him in Quincy, Massachusetts. William's son Arthur fathered three sons, Paul, Arthur and Mark, and through them the modern branches of the family run down to the present generation.

The line behind John. The line is confirmed from John back to Capt. Joseph Read. A deed-by-deed history of John's homestead and the Historical Society of Cheshire County's Reed file name John's father outright, cite Isaiah's will, and preserve a handwritten extract from the vital records giving John's parents in a single line. Westford's vital records confirm the generations above Isaiah: Joshua, born in 1737 to Joseph and Ruth, and Isaiah, born in 1778 to Joshua and Mary. What is left open is the colonial end above Joseph: two links that still rest on the published genealogies, and Thomas himself. The documents behind every claim on this site are gathered on the Documents page.

Stoddard and Reed Hill

The documented line is rooted in Stoddard, New Hampshire, a hill town in Cheshire County. John Reed's family lived there, James Edwin was born there in 1846, and the register page attached to James's profile lists the family at Stoddard.

The landscape still carries the name: Reed Hill is a real named summit in Stoddard, about a mile from the town center. Whether the hill took its name from this family is exactly the kind of question the deed and probate records may answer.

John's own place is pinned down by the Stoddard documents. His homestead stood on top of Jackson Hill, 2,060 feet above sea level, about a quarter mile south of the Stoddard–Washington line and a mile and a half east of the Marlow line. When Arlene Rasmussen walked the site in 1976 the house was long gone, but the L-shaped cellar hole was still there, 26 feet along its longest side, with the brick of the chimney piled inside it, a filled-in well across the road, the stone walls of the barn behind it, and old apple trees at the fork of the dirt road.

Stoddard is also where the trail north begins. The Society file and Rasmussen's history agree that John's father Isaiah came up from Westford, Massachusetts, appearing on Stoddard's tax list in 1800, and that two of Isaiah's older brothers were in Stoddard before him, on lots their grandfather deeded them in 1782. The Stoddard Reeds were a New Hampshire branch of a much older Massachusetts line.

The people

Brief notes on each person in the line, oldest first, with the spouses who married into the family.

Thomas Read, the captive

c. 1656 – 1730/36a tailor of Chelmsford, Massachusetts

Thomas was a soldier in King Philip's War and spent part of it as a captive. Mary Rowlandson met him in captivity and wrote about it in her narrative, published in 1682, only six years after the events: in the chapter called the Thirteenth Remove, she describes Thomas in tears, fearing he would be killed, and records that he told her he had recently seen her husband alive. It is rare, firsthand, near-contemporary evidence that Thomas was real and was there.

On May 15, 1676, Thomas escaped and reached Hatfield, Massachusetts, carrying intelligence about the encampments near the falls of the Connecticut River. The Reverend John Russell described the escape in a letter dated that same day. Four days later, on May 19, Captain William Turner's force attacked the encampment at Peskeompskut, remembered as the Battle of Turner's Falls. Whether Thomas personally fought in the attack is a step beyond what the escape records alone show, but The Reade Record treats his presence at the Falls Fight as certain, citing the June 1736 township grant lists: "Thomas Read, Westford" appears on the approved list, and the claimants include "John Reed, Westford, son of Thomas Reed," an entry that is itself a primary father-and-son record. Thomas was made a freeman on April 1, 1679, settled at Chelmsford as a tailor, and married Hannah Blanchard around 1679. He died sometime between September 1730 and June 1736. One correction the records force: a death date of March 1727 appears in some trees, the family's Ancestry tree among them when it was checked in October 2026, but the deed of September 30, 1730 still styles his son "Thomas Read Junior," a styling that only holds while the father lives. Thomas was alive in 1730, so the 1727 date cannot stand.

Thomas Read Jr.

c. 1687–1773Westford, Massachusetts

Son of Thomas and Hannah. Thomas Jr. married Sarah Fletcher on March 14, 1709/10 and became part of the community of Westford, where he died on December 24, 1773. The Reade Record points to two original records that anchor this generation in primary evidence: the Middlesex court proceeding of May 10, 1725 naming Thomas's sons as John, Thomas, William, Jonathan and Benjamin, and a deed dated September 30, 1730 that still calls him "Thomas Read Junior," showing his father was then living.

Capt. Joseph Read

b. June 4, 1716Westford, MassachusettsDocumented

Joseph is a documented person in his own right: the record gives his birth on June 4, 1716 and his marriage to Ruth Underwood on May 30, 1737, and Westford's vital records name Joseph and Ruth as the parents of Joshua, which anchors him in a primary source. The one link that is not yet proven by an original record is his parentage: that he was Thomas Jr.'s son comes from the genealogy and Westford Historical Society research, and it is that step, not Joseph himself, that stays on the research line. Joseph became a central figure in Westford's civic and political life in the Revolutionary period, with activity tied to the Massachusetts Provincial Congress. His political role is well regarded locally. One famous claim attached to him is not settled, and it is flagged below.

Joshua Read

b. December 1, 1737Westford, Massachusetts

Son of Joseph and Ruth: Westford's own vital records say so, which gives this link a primary source independent of the published genealogies and of the family's research. The same records, and the Westford Historical Society, document his marriage to Mary Spaulding on May 27, 1758. When the Lexington alarm came on April 19, 1775, Joshua marched in Captain Jonathan Minot's militia company, putting him among the Massachusetts men who mobilized on the first day of the Revolutionary War.

His war service was militia only. The Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War roster lists him for three days' service on the April 19, 1775 alarm in Capt. Minot's company, and records no Continental enlistment for him.

Joshua Read Jr.

b. March 6, 1763Westford, Massachusetts · Isaiah's older brother

Son of Joshua and Mary, per Westford's vital records, and the second generation of the family to serve in the Revolution. He enlisted at sixteen. The Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War roster records Continental service for him: nine months in 1779–1780 with Col. Jackson's 8th Massachusetts, followed by a six-month levy through the end of 1780. He matters to this page because an early draft of the line placed him as a possible extra generation between Joshua and Isaiah. The Westford records settle it: Isaiah, born in 1778, was Joshua and Mary's son too, fifteen years Joshua Jr.'s junior. He is a brother in the line, not a link in it.

Elnathan Read

1758–1841Westford, Massachusetts · Isaiah's older brotherDocumented

Son of Joshua Read and Mary Spaulding, and Isaiah's older brother: in Paul's family tree he stands as the present generation's 4th great-granduncle. Gould's History of Stoddard lists an Elnathan Reed among the Revolutionary soldiers who lived in Stoddard after the war, and on that listing alone this site once filed him with Stoddard's other, unrelated Reeds. The tree says otherwise: the soldier in Stoddard was one of ours, a Westford Read who came north ahead of his younger brother Isaiah. He married Anne Prescott and died in 1841 at Cavendish, Vermont. Anne's own family, one of the founding families of Lancaster, Massachusetts, is traced on the Prescotts page.

His Revolutionary War service is set out in the Massachusetts Soldiers and Sailors of the Revolutionary War roster. He served in the militia in 1776, then took a full three-year Continental enlistment as a Private in Capt. Ballard's company, Col. Alden's Massachusetts regiment (afterwards the 7th Massachusetts), from May 1777 to May 1780. The roster places him at Cherry Valley, New York in 1779.

Isaiah Read

b. March 17, 1778Westford to StoddardDocumented

The bridge generation, and the strongest link in the line. Westford's own vital records give Isaiah's birth on March 17, 1778 to Joshua and Mary, and his marriage to Lydia Procter on February 12, 1799, so his place in the line rests on a primary source, not on the genealogies. Westford records no births for Isaiah and Lydia's children, which fits what happened next: the couple moved to Stoddard, New Hampshire right after the wedding, and the children were born there.

FamilySearch's profile for Isaiah lists the seven children of Isaiah and Lydia: Lydia, Isaiah, Jonas P., Nancy, Cyrus, John and Edward. Gould's Stoddard history fills in the town years: Isaiah appears as "Isaiah Reed, Esq.," and in 1848 two dwelling houses, a wood house and a carriage house owned by Isaiah and his son burned. Gould's marriage lists catch two of the children, Jonas P. to Ann Waldron on October 20, 1831, and Nancy to Samuel Jenkins; the Society's Reed file adds that Nancy married a second time, which resolves records that seemed to conflict. Gould also records Isaiah's own second marriage, to Lucinda Dudley on August 12, after Lydia's death in 1857.

Two late pieces of his story come from the Stoddard documents. His will (Cheshire County Probate No. 427, 1868) left ten dollars each to Jonas, John, Nancy and his grandson Columbus, and the homestead to Edward: the document that ties John to this household in Isaiah's own probate. And late in life Isaiah was excommunicated from Stoddard's Congregational church for slipping off to Universalist meetings, while his son Jonas P. sat as a deacon of the church that put him out. Isaiah died on March 13, 1868 and is buried in Joslin Cemetery, Stoddard.

John Reed

b. February 28, 1817 – d. October 5, 18663rd great-grandfatherDocumented

The earliest Reed the family can stand behind without qualification. John farmed on Jackson Hill in Stoddard, New Hampshire: on March 8, 1842 he paid Peter Wright $2,000 for the homestead parcel, some 175 acres in lots 26, 27 and 28 of range 13, and the farm stayed in Reed hands for 58 years. He died of typhoid on October 5, 1866, aged 49 years, 7 months and 5 days, leaving no will. His estate inventory (Cheshire County Probate No. 414) values his livestock at $306.34: 13 oxen, one cow and 10 sheep, plus provisions, farming tools, furniture, and 85 sap buckets that suggest he made maple sugar to sell as well as to eat.

His parentage is no longer a lead. Arlene Rasmussen's deed-by-deed history of his homestead states it outright, twice: "John Reed's father, Isaiah Reed." Isaiah's will (Cheshire County Probate No. 427, 1868) left ten dollars each to Jonas, John, Nancy and his grandson Columbus, with the homestead going to son Edward, and the same history calls Jonas P. Reed "brother of John Reed," which fits, since Jonas is confirmed as Isaiah's son. Strongest of all, a handwritten vital-records extract in the Society's Reed file reads: "John Reed died 5 Oct 1866 from typhoid age 49 yrs. 7 mos 5 days. parents Isaiah and Lydia Reed." The file also lists John, born February 28, 1817, among Isaiah and Lydia's children in three separate notes. John had five sons himself: James Edwin (b. 1846), Henry (b. July 20, 1850), Frank J. (b. August 8, 1853), Albert B. and Fred W. Frank and Henry kept stores in Stoddard Center, and Albert bought the old homestead farm from his mother in 1890.

Wife: Harriet Wright (1826–1907), daughter of Peter Wright (1791–1870) and Diadamia Waldron (1802–1850). One open question hangs over her name: a Stoddard town record says John married "Hannah, daughter of Peter H. and Diadama Wright," while every Reed family record calls her Harriet, and Peter and Diadamia's listed children include neither name. See the Documents page. Her father deeded her the homestead land in 1852 "in consideration of the love and affection I bear to my daughter Harriet Reed, wife of John Reed." Widowed in 1866, she bought the farm back in 1869 and held it for two more decades, then remarried: James Simonds, in 1871.

The colonial generations, in the records

The paper behind the top of the line: the Rowlandson passage that names Thomas Read in captivity, the Falls Fight roster that places him among Captain Turner's men, and the pensioner census that puts Elnathan Read at Cavendish, Vermont at the end of his life. After the documents come the confirmation of the Isaiah to John link, and the two claims this site keeps flagged as disputed.

Isaiah to John: confirmed in the records

This link rests on primary sources, not on the trees. Arlene Rasmussen's history of John's homestead states "John Reed's father, Isaiah Reed," and calls Jonas P. Reed John's brother. Isaiah's will (Cheshire County Probate No. 427, 1868) names John among the children it leaves money to. And a handwritten vital-records extract in the Society's Reed file gives John's death and his parents in a single line: "parents Isaiah and Lydia Reed." Any one of these would be strong; together they settle it.

FamilySearch's public profile for Isaiah (person L4J2-PF1), checked in October 2026, lists the same seven children with John, born 1817, among them, and carries records attached, including Edward's 1819 New Hampshire birth record naming Isaiah as father. The trees agree with the documents; they no longer have to carry the claim alone. The full extracts are on the Documents page.

Disputed: Thomas was not Obadiah's son

Some older genealogies, and many online family trees that copy them, make Thomas Read the son of Obadiah Read and Anna Swift. The dates do not allow it. Obadiah and Anna married in 1664, and their son Thomas was born in July 1665, which would have made him about eleven years old during King Philip's War. The captive Thomas, by contrast, was about twenty in 1676, born around 1656, based on historical court depositions.

The Reade Record challenges the Obadiah connection on exactly these grounds: Thomas's own court depositions put him at age 28 in April 1684 and age 40 in December 1696, both landing on a birth around 1656. Stearns, in Early Generations of the Founders of Old Dunstable (1911), is blunt: Thomas Read of Chelmsford was born about 1656, "parentage unknown," and was not a son of Obadiah and Anna, "as stated in Read Genealogy, 1861." That 1861 Jacob Whittemore Reed genealogy is where the error starts, and even the family's 1915 book repeats it for Thomas's generation, giving him a July 1665 birth as Obadiah's son. Where the 1915 book and the Reade Record conflict on Thomas Sr., the Reade Record wins: its list of his children matches the 1725 court record, and the 1915 book's does not. Any tree that shows Obadiah, or Esdras Read behind him, as this family's ancestor should be treated as unproven, and Thomas Sr.'s parentage is honestly recorded as unknown. A second unverified pairing circulates as well: the family's Ancestry tree, checked in October 2026, names George Reade and Margaret Bird as Thomas's parents. No record supporting that pairing has been found either, so it stands in the same place as the Obadiah claim: a name in a tree, not a documented parent.

Disputed: Capt. Joseph and the Boston Tea Party

The Westford Historical Society recognizes Capt. Joseph Read as a participant in the Boston Tea Party. Researchers at the Boston Tea Party Ships & Museum take a stricter line: the surviving documentary evidence does not conclusively establish that Joseph was physically present when the tea was destroyed on December 16, 1773, and they have questioned how likely his participation was. Joseph's standing in Westford's Revolutionary-era civic life is not in doubt. His presence on Griffin's Wharf that night stays marked disputed until a primary record settles it.

The evidence hunt

The records that would turn the research line into documented fact, in rough order of value. Two pulls have already landed, Isaiah's probate and John's death record, both among the family's Stoddard documents. What is left sits at the colonial end of the line, at Thomas Sr. himself, and on one side thread, the Goulds.

  1. 1

    Middlesex court proceeding, May 10, 1725

    The court record naming Thomas Read's sons: John, Thomas, William, Jonathan and Benjamin, described as men of great estate, while Thomas himself appears as "an ancient and infirm man." Cited by The Reade Record. Note: this is a court proceeding, not a probate. Thomas was still alive in 1730, so any probate or administration for him falls in the 1730 to 1736 window, in the Middlesex probate docket books.

  2. 2

    Middlesex County deed, September 30, 1730

    A land deed identifying Thomas Read Jr., in the digitized Middlesex deed books for 1730 on FamilySearch. The "Junior" styling also shows his father was still living in 1730. Together with the 1725 court proceeding, it would anchor the Thomas to Thomas Jr. link in original records. A companion pull from Stearns: a 1717 deed from Thomas Read to Joseph Blanchard, for land Hannah inherited from her grandfather Joseph Hills and her father Deacon John Blanchard.

  3. 3

    John Reed's birth record, Stoddard, 1817

    No longer the key. In New Hampshire Birth Records, Early to 1900. John's brother Edward (b. 1819) has a record in this set naming Isaiah as father, and it is attached to Isaiah's FamilySearch profile. John's own record still has not surfaced in the public index, and the images need a free FamilySearch sign-in to browse, but the question it was meant to answer is settled: the vital-records extract in the Society's Reed file names John's parents directly (item 5 below). Pulling the birth record itself is now a completeness item, not a proof item.

  4. 4

    Isaiah Read's probate, 1868

    Found. Isaiah's will is Cheshire County Probate No. 427 (1868), cited in Rasmussen's Homestead history: ten dollars each to Jonas, John, Nancy and his grandson Columbus, with the homestead to son Edward. It names John as Isaiah's son from the New Hampshire side, exactly the corroboration this pull was meant to find. The will itself, in the Cheshire County probate books browsable through the FamilySearch catalog, is still worth pulling for the file.

  5. 5

    John Reed's death record, 1866

    Found. Death records of the era often name the deceased's parents, and John's does. A handwritten vital-records extract in the Society's Reed file reads: "John Reed died 5 Oct 1866 from typhoid age 49 yrs. 7 mos 5 days. parents Isaiah and Lydia Reed." It answers the Isaiah question directly, and the age at death fixes John's birth at February 28, 1817.

  6. 6

    Town, church and militia records

    Chelmsford and Westford vital records, church records, land deeds and militia rolls, generation by generation, to firm up every link in the colonial line rather than resting it on the published genealogies alone.

  7. 7

    The Gould thread

    The town history this research leans on was written by a Gould: Isaiah Gould, History of Stoddard, 1897. Goulds were in Stoddard in Isaiah Reed's day and earlier, and this family has a Gould of its own: Paul's grandmother Ethel Lois Gould, wife of Arthur Reed, who married into the family in the twentieth century. The family believes the Reeds and the Stoddard Goulds also married back in the 1700s and 1800s. No record of an early Reed and Gould marriage has been pulled yet, and whether Ethel's Goulds connect to the Stoddard Goulds is unproven. Stoddard's marriage records and the Gould entries in the town history are the place to check.

The Prescotts

The marriage that joins the families

Westford, Massachusetts · 1781Documented

Westford's records hold the 1781 marriage of Elnathan Read and Anne Prescott, and that one entry ties two of Massachusetts's old families together. Elnathan's brothers went on to Stoddard, New Hampshire: Isaiah founded the line this site documents, and Joshua Jr. served in the Revolution's last years. Elnathan's own branch took a different road, west into Vermont, and it is through him, not through Isaiah, that the Prescotts connect to this family.

Westford to Cavendish, Vermont

Elnathan and Anne's branch

Elnathan served in the Continental Army through the Revolution, and after the war the couple put down roots at Cavendish, Vermont, where Elnathan died. A federal record places him there at the end of his life: the 1840 Census of Pensioners for Revolutionary and military services lists Elnathan Read at Cavendish, aged 85. Anne had died on December 6, 1821.

Jonas Jr. and the Stony Brook forge

Westford, Massachusetts

Anne's great-great-grandfather Jonas Prescott Jr. (b. 1678) owned an iron forge on Stony Brook in Westford. The detail matters because it plants the Westford Prescotts in the town's working landscape two generations before Anne: when she married Elnathan Read in 1781, she was marrying within a town both families had belonged to for decades, not arriving in it.

Colonel William Prescott: a cousin, not an ancestor

Bunker Hill, 1775

The famous Prescott of the Revolution, Colonel William Prescott, who commanded the American redoubt at Bunker Hill, descends from the same John Prescott of Lancaster. He belongs to a different branch of the family, though: he is a distant cousin of Anne's Westford line, not a direct ancestor of it, and this page keeps him where the genealogy puts him, as kin at a remove rather than a name to claim.

Open: Elnathan's last years, and his children

Two points on this branch do not yet line up, and both are flagged rather than smoothed over. The family tree gives Elnathan's death as 1841; the published Prescott genealogy gives 1845, but its own age figures do not support that year, and the 1840 pensioner census has him alive at Cavendish either way. The children disagree too: the genealogy lists six children of Elnathan and Anne, born between 1783 and 1792, while the family tree lists two, born in 1798 and 1800. Neither set has been checked yet against Cavendish town records or Elnathan's pension file, which is the pull that would settle both questions.

The documents

Everything this site claims rests on paper, and this page gathers the paper in one place: the documents the family actually holds, the key extracts quoted from them, and every source used or cited anywhere on the site.

The family's papers hold two sets of Stoddard documents. The first is Arlene Rasmussen's deed-by-deed history, "The John Reed Homestead," which traces the Jackson Hill farm from John's 1842 purchase forward and names his father outright. The second is the Historical Society of Cheshire County's Reed file, a 134-page research packet the Society compiled in 2015 from what one Reed family had gathered on the Stoddard Reeds: register pages, handwritten vital-records extracts, a descendant chart, and notes from the town's own records.

Read together, the two sets confirm the link the line stands on: John Reed was Isaiah Reed's son, and from John the line runs confirmed back to Capt. Joseph Read. The proofs are set out below, with the documents reproduced as found: the two Stoddard papers first, then the two pages from the published Prescott genealogy that carry Anne Prescott's marriage and her children, then the military records: the Rowlandson passage that names Thomas Read in captivity, Bodge's Falls Fight roster, and the 1840 pensioner census entry for Elnathan. "John Reed's father, Isaiah Reed"

"The John Reed Homestead," Arlene Rasmussen

Rasmussen's history of the Jackson Hill property traces the farm deed by deed from John's 1842 purchase, and names his father outright, twice: "John Reed's father, Isaiah Reed." The same history calls Jonas P. Reed "brother of John Reed," which lines up with the records, because Jonas is confirmed as Isaiah's son.

Isaiah's will, 1868

Cheshire County Probate No. 427

The Homestead history cites Isaiah's will, proved in 1868, and what it left to the family:

Ten dollars each to "Jonas, John, Nancy and his grandson Columbus," with the homestead going to son Edward.

John is named in his father's will alongside his brother, his sister and his nephew. Wills name children; this one names John.

The vital-records extract

Handwritten extract, Society Reed file

The strongest single piece of paper in the packet: a handwritten extract from the vital records, independent of the Homestead history, giving John's death and his parents in one line:

"John Reed died 5 Oct 1866 from typhoid age 49 yrs. 7 mos 5 days. parents Isaiah and Lydia Reed."

The age works back to a birth on February 28, 1817, which corrects the February 22 date the family tree used to carry.

Color from the packet

Society Reed file, 134 pages

The packet filled in lives as well as links. Isaiah, late in life, was excommunicated from Stoddard's Congregational church for slipping away to Universalist meetings, while his son Jonas P. sat as a deacon of that same church. John's sister Nancy married twice, which resolves a standing conflict between records that seemed to give her two different husbands. And John's widow Harriet remarried in 1871, to James Simonds.

Open: was her name Hannah or Harriet?

The packet raised one question it could not answer. A Stoddard town record says John Reed married "Hannah, daughter of Peter H. and Diadama Wright." Every Reed family record, including the 1852 deed from Peter Wright himself, calls John's wife Harriet. Peter and Diadamia Wright's listed children include neither a Hannah nor a Harriet. So the first name of the family's 3rd great-grandmother is not certain: she is Harriet in the family records and Hannah in the town's, and the Wright children's list backs neither. It stays open until a record that names her plainly turns up.

The other Reeds of Stoddard: no relation

Stoddard had Reeds before ours arrived. Gould's History of Stoddard (1897) records a Reed presence in town going back to the Revolutionary generation: Joshua Reed, a soldier who lived in Stoddard after the war and whose name is still on Reed's Pond. The oldest gravestone Gould found in the town burying ground, dated January 1, 1792, marks a child of that Joshua. All of this predates Isaiah's move up from Westford in 1799, and no record connects the earlier households to his. That is worth saying precisely: what the records show is an absence of any link, not a proof that none could exist. If a document ever connects them, this page gets corrected. One correction of exactly that kind has already landed: Elnathan Reed, whom Gould lists among Stoddard's Revolutionary soldiers, sits in Paul's own family tree as a son of Joshua Read and Mary Spaulding of Westford, Isaiah's older brother, so he is family and now stands with the line on the Family line page, not here.

They get a page of their own because the trap is a real one. Same small town, same surname, and even the same first names: Stoddard's Joshua Reed invites a merge with Joshua Read of Westford, Isaiah's father, and a tree built on name matches alone will make that merge without blinking. Below is what the records actually say about each of them, kept apart from our line, followed by the working rule for telling the two families apart.

Joshua Reed, of Reed's Pond

early Stoddard settlerNo relation

Gould names Joshua Reed among the Revolutionary soldiers who afterwards lived in Stoddard, a generation before Isaiah arrived from Westford in 1799. The town still carried his name in Gould's day: "Reed's pond, located near where Joshua Reed once resided, and still bears his name." The oldest gravestone Gould found in the town burying ground, dated January 1, 1792, marks a child of this Joshua Reed.

The trap to avoid: do not merge him with Joshua Read of Westford (b. 1737), Isaiah's father. The two men share a name and a war, but no record ties Stoddard's Joshua to the Westford line, and his family was already burying children in Stoddard by 1792, years before Isaiah arrived.

Asaph Reed

South StoddardNo relation

Gould's list of the town's accidental deaths holds one more Reed who is not ours: "Asaph Reed was frozen to death going home from the village in South Stoddard, aged 49." No wife, children or origin are given, and no record places him in Isaiah's family.

Jesse Reed, and the Reed marriages

Stoddard, 1820s–1830sNo relation

The marriage lists Gould prints show a Reed household running parallel to Isaiah's in the same years. Jesse Reed married Sally McIntire, and then, on March 15, 1832, Clarissa McIntire. Catharine Reed married Thomas Colburn on April 19, 1825, and Sally Reed married Thomas Sanderson on June 2, 1825. Olive Reed married Benjamin Jefts, and Sophronia Reed married Jonathan Clark. None of these names appear among Isaiah and Lydia's seven children, and the two sets of Reeds should be kept apart in any tree.

How to tell the two Stoddard Reed families apart

Our line's Stoddard paper trail runs through Isaiah: the Westford marriage in 1799, the children born in Stoddard after the move, the houses he and a son lost to fire in 1848, his second marriage to Lucinda Dudley after Lydia's death in 1857, and his burial in Joslin Cemetery in 1868. The other line is older in town and thinner in the records: a Revolutionary soldier's pond, a child's stone dated 1792, and a scatter of marriages. If a Stoddard Reed cannot be placed in Isaiah's household, the working assumption is that they belong to the other line, not ours.

A note on the name. The colonial generations spelled it Read: Thomas Read, Joseph Read, Joshua Read. The Stoddard line and the modern family spell it Reed. Both spellings appear in the records for the same people, and this page keeps each generation's own spelling where the sources give one.
